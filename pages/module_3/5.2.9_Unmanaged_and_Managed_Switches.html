5.2.9 Unmanaged and Managed Switches

Unmanaged vs. Managed Switches

Feature Unmanaged Managed
Configuration required None - plug and play Optional via management port (web or CLI)
Typical port count 4â€“8 ports 24 or 48 access ports
Use case Small/home networks, SOHO routers Workgroups, corporate/enterprise LANs
Rack-mountable Typically no Yes, standard network racks
Uplink ports Typically no Yes, for connecting to other switches
Advanced features No Yes (security settings, VLANs, etc.)

A managed switch works as an unmanaged switch out-of-the-box. Advanced features are enabled by an administrator via a management port.

Modular Switches

Enterprise networks may use modular switches - a chassis with a shared power supply and high-speed backplane that interconnects multiple switch modules. This allows hundreds of access ports to be provisioned through a single compact appliance.

Key Terms

Unmanaged switch
A plug-and-play switch requiring no configuration. Typically 4â€“8 ports; used in small or home networks. Embedded in most ISP-supplied SOHO router/modems.
Managed switch
A switch that supports administrator configuration via a management port (web interface or CLI). Used in workgroup and enterprise environments; typically rack-mounted with 24 or 48 ports.
Uplink port
A higher-speed port on a managed switch used to connect it to other switches, enabling network expansion.
Modular switch
An enterprise switch chassis that houses multiple switch modules sharing a common power supply and backplane, enabling hundreds of ports in a single appliance.
