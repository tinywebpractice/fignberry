5.3.11 Optical Cabling

Fiber Optic vs. Copper

Copper carries electrical signals that are sensitive to interference and attenuation. Fiber optic cables carry light pulses (generated by lasers or LEDs) that are immune to interference and suffer less attenuation, enabling much higher bandwidth (multi-Gbps to Tbps) and longer runs (miles vs. feet).

Fiber Optic Cable Construction

  • Core: ultra-fine glass that carries the light pulses.
  • Cladding: glass or plastic layer surrounding the core that guides light pulses along it.
  • Buffer: protective coating over the cladding.
  • Jacket: outer protective sheath enclosing the whole fiber.

SMF vs. MMF

Feature Single-Mode Fiber (SMF) Multimode Fiber (MMF)
Core diameter 8-10 microns 50 or 62.5 microns
Wavelength 1,310 nm or 1,550 nm 850 nm or 1,300 nm
Light source High-power laser diode LED or VCSEL
Speed 10 Gbps or better Lower than SMF
Distance Many kilometers Shorter than SMF
Cost Higher Lower
Best use WANs and long-distance runs LANs

Fiber Optic Connectors

All fiber optic connectors use a ceramic or plastic ferrule to ensure continuous alignment of the light signal. Patch cords can use the same connector type on both ends or a mix (e.g. ST-SC). Connectors are fragile and should not be repeatedly plugged and unplugged. Cover unused ports with dust caps.

Connector Full Name Mechanism Notes
ST Straight-tip connector Push-and-twist (bayonet) Older; used mostly on multi-mode networks.
SC Subscriber connector Push/pull Simplex and duplex versions (duplex = two connectors clipped together). Single- or multi-mode.
LC Lucent connector (also: little-connector, local-connector) Tabbed push/pull Small form factor; higher port density than SC. Single- or multi-mode.

Key Terms

SMF (Single-Mode Fiber)
Fiber with an 8-10 micron core carrying a 1,310 or 1,550 nm laser signal. Supports 10+ Gbps over many kilometers. Used for long-distance and WAN links.
MMF (Multimode Fiber)
Fiber with a 50 or 62.5 micron core carrying 850 or 1,300 nm light from an LED or VCSEL. Lower cost and distance than SMF; suited for LANs.
VCSEL (Vertical Cavity Surface Emitting Laser)
A low-cost laser light source used with multimode fiber.
Ferrule
The ceramic or plastic tip of a fiber optic connector that aligns the fiber core for continuous light transmission.
