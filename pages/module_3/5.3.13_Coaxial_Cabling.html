5.3.13 Coaxial Cabling

Coaxial Cable Overview

Coaxial (coax) cable is a copper cable that carries electrical signals using two conductors sharing the same axis. Unlike twisted pair (which uses balanced signaling to cancel interference), coax uses a wire mesh outer conductor as both an EMI shield and a ground.

Coax construction from inside out: center signal conductor, plastic insulation (dielectric), wire mesh conductor (shield/ground), outer jacket.

Current uses: CCTV installations, Cable Access TV (CATV) patch cabling, broadband cable modems.

Key Terms

Coaxial cable
A copper cable with a center signal conductor surrounded by dielectric insulation, a wire mesh outer conductor (EMI shield and ground), and an outer jacket. Used for CCTV, CATV, and cable modems.
Dielectric
The plastic insulation layer between the center conductor and the outer mesh conductor in coaxial cable.
F-type connector
A screw-down coaxial connector used for CATV and cable modem connections.
BNC (Bayonet Neill-Concelman)
A coaxial connector using a bayonet coupling mechanism (push and twist to lock). Used in video, radio, and television cabling.
