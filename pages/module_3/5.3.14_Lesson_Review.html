5.3.14 Lesson Review - Q&A

Q1. A network technician needs to deploy a computer on an Ethernet network and notices that the computer will sit on a shelf directly under a fluorescent light. What type of cable will the technician use?

  • UTP
  • STP
  • Fiber optic
  • Coax

Fluorescent lights are a known source of EMI. STP is required in high-interference environments. UTP has no shielding, fiber is immune but not used for standard desktop Ethernet connections, and coax is not used for Ethernet workstation connections.

Q2. A network technician is installing a device with an inductor that will copy all the traffic coming through the cable to a monitor port. What type of device is the technician installing?

  • SMF
  • Active TAP
  • MMF
  • Passive TAP

A passive TAP uses an inductor (or optical splitter) to physically copy signals to a monitor port with no powered logic. An active TAP performs signal regeneration and does not use an inductor. SMF and MMF are fiber optic cable types, not monitoring devices.

Q3. You are installing network cabling in the air space between the ceiling and the roof of a building. The cabling needs to be fire-resistant. Which type of cabling should you use?

  • STP
  • Multi-mode fiber
  • Plenum
  • PVC

The air space between ceiling and roof is a plenum space used for HVAC airflow, which makes it an effective fire conduit. Building codes require CMP-rated plenum cable there. PVC is specifically not allowed in plenum spaces.

Q4. What distinguishes single-mode fiber (SMF) from multi-mode fiber (MMF) in terms of usage?

  • SMF has a larger core than MMF, allowing for more light to pass through.
  • SMF uses lower-cost LEDs, while MMF uses high-power lasers.
  • SMF and MMF perform identically, with only minor cost differences.
  • SMF is better suited for long distances and high speeds, whereas MMF is ideal for shorter distances in LANs.

SMF has the smaller core (8-10 microns) and uses high-power laser diodes for long-distance, high-speed runs. MMF has the larger core (50 or 62.5 microns) and uses cheaper LEDs/VCSELs for shorter LAN runs. The first two options have the core size and light source relationships reversed.

Q5. A network technician used a tool that energizes each wire in a cable. When the technician sent energy to some of the wires, the LED on the tool did not light up. What tool was the technician using?

  • Tone generator
  • Loopback plug
  • Toner probe
  • Cable tester

A cable tester energizes each wire in turn and uses LEDs to indicate continuity at each pin. An unlit LED means that wire is not conducting, pointing to a bad termination or damaged insulation. A tone generator applies an audio signal to identify a cable in a bundle. A loopback plug tests a port, not individual wires.

Q6. What is a major benefit STP provides that UTP does not?

  • Safer for installations in overhead ceiling spaces
  • Lower cost
  • Greater resistance to interference
  • Ability to install longer cable lengths

STP's shielding protects against EMI and crosstalk in high-interference environments. UTP is actually the lower-cost option. Plenum-rated cable (not STP) addresses ceiling safety. Both STP and UTP share the same 100 m maximum distance limitation.

Q7. Your workstation is unable to communicate with any other computer on the network. Which of the following tools should you use to test the network card's abilities to send and receive signals?

  • Cable tester
  • ping command
  • Loopback plug
  • Multimeter

A loopback plug tests whether a NIC port can send and receive by crossing the transmit and receive pins (1-to-3, 2-to-6). A solid link LED confirms the port is functional. A cable tester checks cable continuity. ping requires network connectivity to already exist. A multimeter measures electrical properties but does not test NIC port function.

Q8. Why might an active network tap be used instead of a passive tap?

  • Because active taps work without power, making them reliable in power outages.
  • Because active taps filter out corrupt or malformed frames before sending data to the monitor.
  • Because active taps are exclusively used for fiber optic connections.
  • Because active taps regenerate signals, which is necessary for complex or high-speed links.

Active TAPs are needed when the signal is too complex for a passive inductor/splitter to copy accurately, specifically Gigabit copper signaling and some fiber links. The first option describes a passive TAP. Active TAPs do not filter frames and are not exclusive to fiber.

Q9. Why can fiber optic cables support higher bandwidth and longer distances compared to copper cables?

  • Copper signals are stronger than light pulses, leading to shorter cable runs.
  • The light pulses in fiber optics are not susceptible to interference and experience less attenuation.
  • Fiber optic cables are thicker, which reduces interference.
  • Fiber optic cables are less durable and, therefore, require longer cable runs.

Light pulses are immune to EMI and suffer far less attenuation than electrical signals, enabling higher bandwidth and longer runs. Physical thickness is not the reason for reduced interference, and durability has nothing to do with cable run distance.

Q10. You are creating patch cables and notice data transmission is not functioning properly in some cables. One cable is interfering with transmission at the near end. What is this type of interference called?

  • Cross-talk interference
  • Router interference
  • Wi-Fi interference
  • Cat 5 Ethernet interference

Crosstalk is interference caused by signals bleeding between adjacent wire pairs. The "near end" detail points specifically to NEXT (Near-End CrossTalk). Router interference, Wi-Fi interference, and Cat 5 Ethernet interference are not recognized terms for this phenomenon.

Q11. You recently purchased a digital HD antenna to connect to your television. Which of the following is the type of coaxial cable connector you need to make the connection?

  • BNC
  • F-type
  • UTP
  • EMI

F-type is the standard screw-down coaxial connector used for TV/antenna and CATV connections. BNC uses a bayonet coupling and is used for professional video/radio equipment. UTP is a cable type. EMI is electromagnetic interference, not a connector.

Q12. A network cable runs across high-traffic floor areas, causing it to wear and break. The replacement cable is plenum-rated and shielded. Which of the following will BEST protect the cable from further damage?

  • Run the cable through the ceiling area instead of across the floor.
  • Encase the cable in a protective shield and secure the cable to prevent it from slipping.
  • Periodically check the cable for kinks and wear. Replace the cable when necessary.
  • Run the cable under the carpet.

Because the replacement cable is plenum-rated, it can be run through the ceiling/plenum space, completely removing it from floor traffic. Encasing it still leaves it on the floor where it will eventually wear out again. Periodic replacement is reactive and costly. Running cable under carpet is a fire hazard.

Q13. What is the primary function of a network tap?

  • To intercept and copy signals passing over a cable for monitoring or analysis.
  • To provide additional ports for connecting multiple network devices.
  • To replace network switches in small networks.
  • To amplify signals passing over a cable for longer transmission distances.

A network tap copies signals to a monitor port for analysis. Adding ports is a switch function. Taps do not replace switches. Signal amplification describes a repeater.

Q14. You need to trace the route that a Cat 6 UTP cable takes through the ceiling and walls of your building. Which tool should you use?

  • Cable tester
  • Loopback plug
  • Tone generator
  • Multimeter

A toner probe (tone generator + probe) is used to trace a cable's physical route through walls and ceilings. The generator applies an audio signal; the probe detects it along the run. A cable tester checks continuity and pin mapping. A loopback plug tests a NIC or port. A multimeter measures electrical properties.

Q15. A technician is running a network above a dropped ceiling that is also used for ventilation. The cable must be a twisted pair and must be capable of Gigabit Ethernet speeds. Which of the following cables should the technician use?

  • Plenum-rated multimode fiber
  • Riser-rated RG-58
  • Riser-rated Cat 5e
  • Plenum-rated Cat 6a

Three requirements narrow this down: plenum-rated (ventilated dropped ceiling = plenum space), twisted pair (rules out fiber and coax), and Gigabit capable. Cat 6a is plenum-rated twisted pair supporting 10 Gbps. Multimode fiber is not twisted pair. RG-58 is coaxial. Riser-rated cable is not approved for plenum spaces.
