5.3.1 Unshielded Twisted Pair

UTP Cable Overview

Unshielded twisted pair (UTP) is the most popular type of network cable. It consists of four copper conductor wire pairs inside an insulated jacket. Two design features work together to reduce interference:

  • Twisted pairs: each pair is twisted at a different rate from the others, which reduces crosstalk and interference between pairs.
  • Balanced signaling: each wire in a pair carries an equal but opposite signal, making it easier to distinguish the intended signal from interference.

Despite these features, UTP is still limited by attenuation (signal strength loss over distance). The maximum recommended segment length is 100 m (328 feet).

Key Terms

UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)
The most common network cable type, consisting of four pairs of twisted copper conductors inside an insulated jacket. Maximum recommended distance: 100 m.
Balanced signaling
A signaling method where each wire in a pair carries an equal but opposite signal, allowing the receiver to distinguish the signal from interference.
Attenuation
The loss of signal strength over distance. The reason UTP cable segments are limited to 100 m.
