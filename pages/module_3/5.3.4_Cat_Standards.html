5.3.4 Cat Standards

Category (Cat) Standards Overview

A Category (Cat) standard defines the performance of twisted-pair cabling. Higher Cat numbers support higher data rates. Specifications are defined in TIA/EIA-568-C and are printed on the cable jacket along with the cable type (e.g. UTP, F/UTP).

Cat Max Speed Max Distance Ethernet Standard Notes
5 100 Mbps 100 m (328 ft) 100BASE-TX (Fast Ethernet) Obsolete; no longer commercially available.
5e 1 Gbps 100 m (328 ft) 1000BASE-T (Gigabit Ethernet) Still acceptable for Gigabit links; most sites now use Cat 6.
6 1 Gbps 100 m (328 ft) 1000BASE-T More reliable than Cat 5e for Gigabit; supports 10 Gbps at reduced range. Current mainstream choice.
10 Gbps 55 m (180 ft) 10GBASE-T
6A 10 Gbps 100 m (328 ft) 10GBASE-T Full 10 Gbps at 100 m. Bulkier/heavier; more stringent install requirements. Recommended for healthcare, 802.3bt PoE, and AP distribution cabling.
7 10 Gbps 100 m (328 ft) 10GBASE-T Not widely deployed. Preferred connectors: GG45 or TERA (not standard RJ45 for full spec).
100 Gbps 15 m (50 ft) 100GBASE-T
8 25 Gbps 30 m (100 ft) 25GBASE-T Mainly for data centers. Cat 8.1: RJ45 (backward compatible). Cat 8.2: GG45/TERA (not backward compatible).
40 Gbps 30 m (100 ft) 40GBASE-T

Practical Guidance

  • Cat 5: obsolete. Networks still using it will need rewiring to support Gigabit Ethernet.
  • Cat 5e: acceptable for Gigabit links but Cat 6 is preferred for new installations.
  • Cat 6: current mainstream standard; more reliable than Cat 5e, and supports 10 Gbps at short range.
  • Cat 6A: recommended by TIA/EIA for healthcare facilities, 802.3bt PoE installations, and AP distribution cabling.
  • Cat 7/8: niche use in data centers and high-bandwidth short-run installations; not common in typical office or home networks.

Key Terms

Cat standard
A TIA/EIA-defined specification printed on cable jackets that describes the performance rating of twisted-pair cabling, including maximum speed and distance.
TIA/EIA-568-C
The Commercial Building Telecommunications Cabling Standard that defines Cat cable specifications.
GG45 / TERA
Connector types used with Cat 7 and Cat 8.2 cabling. GG45 is backward compatible with RJ45 plugs but may not deliver full Cat 7 performance with a standard RJ45 jack.
Cat 8.1 vs. Cat 8.2
Cat 8.1 uses RJ45 connectors and is backward compatible with standard RJ45 systems. Cat 8.2 uses GG45 or TERA connectors and is not backward compatible.
