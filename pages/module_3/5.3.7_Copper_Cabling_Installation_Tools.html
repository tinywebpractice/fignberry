5.3.7 Copper Cabling Installation Tools

Structured Cabling Termination Types

Cable Type Termination Method Wire Type Max Length
Patch cord RJ45 plug crimped to end Stranded (flexible, more attenuation) 5 m
Permanent cable IDC punchdown at wall port / patch panel Solid (thicker, less attenuation) Remainder of 100 m channel link

The 100 m limit applies to the full channel link (permanent cable + patch cords combined).

Installation Tools

Tool Purpose Key Notes
Cable stripper Removes the outer jacket to expose wire pairs Set to correct diameter; rotate once or twice to score the jacket without damaging inner insulation.
Snips (electrician's scissors) Cuts the plastic star filler (Cat 6/6A) and excess ripcord before termination Star filler keeps pairs separated inside the cable. Ripcord can open the jacket further if inner pairs are damaged.
Punchdown tool Presses conductors into IDC terminals at wall ports and patch panels Arrange wires in T568A or T568B order. Untwist no more than 1/2" (13 mm) per pair to minimize interference. IDC blades cut through insulation to make contact.
Crimper Attaches an RJ45 plug to a patch cord Orient plug with tab latch down; Pin 1 on the left. Arrange wires in T568A or T568B order, push into plug, then crimp to pierce insulation at pins and seal jacket.

Key Terms

Channel link
The full end-to-end cabling run including permanent cable and all patch cords. Maximum length is 100 m.
Solid cable
Cable with a single thick conductor per wire, used for permanent runs. Less flexible but lower attenuation than stranded.
Stranded cable
Cable with multiple thin conductors twisted together per wire, used for patch cords. More flexible but higher attenuation.
Star filler
A plastic cross-shaped insert in Cat 6 and Cat 6A cable that keeps the wire pairs physically separated to reduce crosstalk. Must be trimmed before termination.
Ripcord
A nylon thread inside the cable jacket that can be pulled to open the jacket further. Excess must be snipped before termination.
T568A / T568B
Two TIA/EIA wiring standards that define the pin-to-pair order for terminating twisted pair cable at RJ45 plugs and IDC connectors.
