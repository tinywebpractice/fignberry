5.3.9 Network Taps

Network Tap Overview

A network tap intercepts signals passing over a cable and forwards them to a packet or protocol analyzer for monitoring. Taps are either passive (unpowered) or active (powered).

Type How It Works Pros Cons
Passive TAP Uses an inductor or optical splitter to physically copy the signal to a monitor port. No logic decisions made. Captures every frame including corrupt/malformed ones; unaffected by network load; no power required. Cannot monitor Gigabit copper signaling (too complex); optical splitting may affect some fiber links.
Active TAP Powered device that regenerates the signal before copying it to a monitor port. Can handle Gigabit copper and fiber links where passive taps fall short. Becomes a point of failure for the monitored link if power is lost.

Switched Port Analysis (SPAN) / Mirror Port

An alternative to a physical tap is a switched port analysis (SPAN) or mirror port configured on a network switch. A sensor attached to the mirror port receives copies of frames from nominated access ports or all other ports on the switch.

Key Terms

Network tap
A device that intercepts and copies signals on a network cable, forwarding them to a packet or protocol analyzer for monitoring.
Passive TAP (test access point)
An unpowered tap that uses an inductor or optical splitter to copy signals. Captures all frames regardless of load, but cannot handle Gigabit copper or some fiber links.
Active TAP
A powered tap that regenerates signals before copying them. Required for Gigabit copper and some fiber links, but becomes a point of failure if power is lost.
SPAN / mirror port (switched port analysis)
A switch feature that copies frames from nominated ports to a designated monitor port, allowing network sniffing without a physical tap.
