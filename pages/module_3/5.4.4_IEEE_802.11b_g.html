5.4.4 IEEE 802.11b/g

802.11b vs. 802.11g Comparison

Feature 802.11b 802.11g
Band 2.4 GHz 2.4 GHz
Max data rate 11 Mbps 54 Mbps
Modulation DSSS (Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum) OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing)
Channel width 22 MHz 20 MHz
Backward compatibility N/A Compatible with 802.11b clients

802.11g uses the same 54 Mbps rate and OFDM encoding as 802.11a, but operates in the 2.4 GHz band. This made backward compatibility with 802.11b straightforward. The channel layout in the 2.4 GHz range is kept the same between b and g for compatibility.

2.4 GHz Channel Layout

The 2.4 GHz band has up to 14 channels spaced 5 MHz apart (2,412 MHz to 2,484 MHz center frequencies). Because each channel is 22 MHz wide (802.11b) or 20 MHz wide (802.11g), channels overlap significantly. To avoid interference, only three non-overlapping channels should be used: 1, 6, and 11.

Region Permitted Channels
Americas 1-11
Europe 1-13
Japan 1-14

Key Terms

DSSS (Direct-Sequence Spread Spectrum)
The modulation technique used by 802.11b. Spreads the signal across a 22 MHz channel to improve resistance to interference.
OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing)
A more efficient modulation technique used by 802.11g (and 802.11a). Sends more data across a 20 MHz channel by dividing it into multiple sub-carriers.
Modulation
The process of modifying a radio wave so it can carry data.
Non-overlapping channels (2.4 GHz)
Channels 1, 6, and 11 are the only three non-overlapping channels in the 2.4 GHz band in the Americas, and should be used to avoid co-channel interference.
