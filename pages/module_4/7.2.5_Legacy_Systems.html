7.2.5 Legacy Systems

Key Terms

Legacy System
A system no longer directly supported by its vendor, due to the vendor going out of business or formally deprecating the product.
EOL (End-of-Life)
Status of a product that is no longer supported by its vendor; no further patches or updates will be issued.

Why Legacy Systems Stay on Networks

  • Run services that are too complex or expensive to migrate to a modern platform.
  • May include legacy OSs, application software, or old-style mainframe computers.
  • Generally still functional for their intended purpose, which is why replacement is deprioritized.

Security Risks and Mitigations

Risk Mitigation
No vendor patches available for discovered exploits Isolate legacy systems as far as possible from the rest of the network
Unpatched vulnerabilities can be exploited by attackers Protect and monitor all network channels linking legacy systems
