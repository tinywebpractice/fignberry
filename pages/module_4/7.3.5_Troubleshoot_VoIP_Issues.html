7.3.5 Troubleshoot VoIP Issues

VoIP vs. Bursty Data

Bursty Data (HTTP, FTP, Email) Real-Time / VoIP
Packet loss Sensitive (retransmission required) Tolerant (some loss acceptable)
Delivery delay / sequence Tolerant Very sensitive
Symptoms of problems Slow transfers Dropouts, echo, glitches in call

Key Terms: Latency and Jitter

Latency
Time for a signal to reach the recipient, measured in ms. Increases with distance and router processing delays. VoIP maximum one-way latency: about 150 ms.
RTT (Round Trip Time)
Two-way latency; time for a host to receive a response to a probe.
Jitter
Variation in delay between packets arriving, measured by sampling elapsed time between packets. VoIP can buffer up to about 30 ms of jitter without severe call quality impact. Typically caused by network congestion at routers and switches.

QoS (Quality of Service)

QoS configures switches, APs, and routers to identify and prioritize VoIP traffic over bursty data. It is the primary solution for ensuring acceptable VoIP call quality.

  • Enterprise: Sophisticated QoS and traffic engineering protocols on managed switches and routers.
  • SOHO: Bandwidth control or QoS feature on the router/modem; prioritize the port used by the VoIP app over other protocols. QoS cannot be guaranteed over a public network such as the internet.

Diagnosing Latency and Jitter

  • Use the router/modem management interface to report latency and jitter.
  • If unavailable, use a speed test site to measure latency and bandwidth.
  • If latency persistently exceeds the agreed service level, contact your ISP.
