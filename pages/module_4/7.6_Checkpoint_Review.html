7.7 Checkpoint Review — Q&A

Question 1

You are an IT technician tasked with setting up a home office network for a client. The client has a DSL internet connection and reports that their phone line experiences interference when they are online. The phone line and DSL modem are connected to the same wall socket without any additional hardware. What is the MOST appropriate solution?

  • Upgrade the client's internet connection to a fiber optic service to avoid using the phone line.
  • Adjust the modem's settings to prioritize voice signals over data signals.
  • Replace the DSL modem with a cable modem to eliminate interference.
  • Install a DSL splitter (filter) to separate voice and data signals on the phone line.

A DSL splitter (microfilter) separates the high-frequency DSL data signal from the low-frequency voice signal on the same phone line, eliminating interference. Fiber is a major infrastructure change for a simple fix. Modem settings cannot physically separate signals. Cable modems require coaxial infrastructure, not phone lines.

Question 2

You have configured Disk 3 and Disk 4 as a RAID 1 array with NTFS, assigned drive letter F, with each disk at 1 TB capacity. Drive F shows only 1 TB available instead of 2 TB. What should you do?

  • Make sure both disks are set to dynamic instead of basic.
  • Nothing. The RAID array is working as designed.
  • Change the volume to a spanned volume.
  • You need to change to a RAID 5 array.

RAID 1 is mirroring — both disks hold identical copies for redundancy. Usable capacity equals one disk (1 TB), not the combined total. Seeing 1 TB is correct behavior. A spanned volume maximizes capacity but provides no redundancy. RAID 5 requires at least 3 disks.

Question 3

A computer transported to a new office location will not power on. Visual inspection reveals a small crack on the case and bent pins on the peripheral connectors. What is the MOST likely cause?

  • The small crack on the computer case has led to internal component misalignment.
  • Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) has damaged the motherboard's soldered chips and components.
  • The computer's power supply unit has failed due to physical impact during transportation.
  • The bent pins on the peripheral connectors are causing a short circuit, preventing the computer from powering on.

The crack indicates the unit took a fall or knock during transport, which could cause internal components (RAM, expansion cards, cables) to become misaligned or disconnected. Bent pins on peripheral connectors typically affect the connected peripherals but do not prevent the entire system from powering on. ESD leaves no visible evidence. PSU failure is possible but the crack provides more direct evidence of internal impact damage.

Question 4

An employee's monitor is powered on but the screen is blank. Which of the following steps should you take next to troubleshoot the missing video issue?

  • Replace the monitor with a new one to see if the problem persists.
  • Immediately update the video card drivers to the latest version.
  • Adjust the monitor's brightness and contrast settings using the OSD menu.
  • Check the cable and connectors between the video card and monitor.

Physical-first troubleshooting: a loose or disconnected video cable is the most common cause of a blank screen on a powered monitor. Hardware replacement and driver updates come after physical checks. Brightness/contrast does not cause a completely blank no-signal screen.

Question 5

A hardware technician is following CompTIA's troubleshooting model for a monitor with a squiggly screen. The technician suspects the cause is fluorescent light interference. What is the next step?

  • Establish a plan of action.
  • Verify full system functionality.
  • Identify the problem.
  • Test the theory.

CompTIA troubleshooting steps: 1) Identify the problem, 2) Establish a theory, 3) Test the theory, 4) Establish a plan of action, 5) Implement solution, 6) Verify full functionality, 7) Document. The problem has been identified and a theory formed; the next step is testing it (e.g., move the monitor away from the light).

Question 6

What function does an Ethernet switch perform when it receives a data frame?

  • It discards the frame if the destination is not in its MAC address table.
  • It stores the frame until the destination device requests it.
  • It decodes the frame and forwards it to the appropriate port based on the destination MAC address.
  • It broadcasts the frame to all ports.

A switch uses its MAC address table to forward frames only to the port where the destination device resides. If the destination MAC is unknown, it floods (not discards) the frame to all ports. Broadcasting to all ports describes a hub. Holding frames for device requests is not switch behavior.

Question 7

When troubleshooting a computer problem, what is the purpose of "Questioning the Obvious"?

  • To skip basic checks and move directly to complex diagnostics.
  • To focus only on software-related issues.
  • To escalate the issue to senior technical staff immediately.
  • To identify and eliminate non-causes systematically.

Questioning the Obvious means checking the simplest possible causes first and ruling them out methodically before moving to complex diagnostics. It applies across all layers, not just software. Escalation is a separate concept.

Question 8

Where should you apply thermal paste when installing a processor?

  • Between the CPU and fan
  • Between the motherboard and CPU
  • Between the heat sink and fan
  • Between the heat sink and CPU

Thermal paste fills microscopic air gaps between the CPU heat spreader and the heat sink base to maximize thermal transfer. The fan attaches mechanically to the heat sink; no paste is used there. Paste on the motherboard socket would damage it.

Question 9

Why does an Ethernet switch eliminate the negative effects of collisions in a network?

  • It limits the amount of data transmitted to avoid congestion.
  • It prevents devices from sending data at the same time.
  • It assigns a separate collision domain to each port.
  • It reduces the number of devices connected to the network.

Each switch port is its own collision domain, so collisions can only occur between a device and that port. This isolates collision effects network-wide. Limiting data describes QoS. Preventing simultaneous transmission describes CSMA/CD on hubs. Reducing device count is not how switches eliminate collisions.

Question 10

An engineer is designing a new computer system and wants to provide active cooling to improve airflow and dissipate heat from around the components. What will the engineer use?

  • Heat sinks
  • Thermal paste
  • Thermal pads
  • Fans

Fans are active cooling devices — they require power and mechanically move air. Heat sinks, thermal paste, and thermal pads are all passive thermal management components with no moving parts.

Question 11

Devices on a SOHO local network can communicate with each other but cannot access the internet. Investigation reveals the router's IPv4 forwarding feature is disabled. What action should you take?

  • Enable IPv4 forwarding on the router.
  • Enable NAT (Network Address Translation) on the router.
  • Configure a new subnet mask for the local network.
  • Assign static IP addresses to all devices on the network.

IPv4 forwarding allows the router to pass packets between the local network and the internet. The cause is already identified; enabling it directly resolves it. Subnet mask and static IPs do not affect routing. NAT may also be needed, but the specific identified cause is IPv4 forwarding being disabled.

Question 12

A user reports that their laptop is not connecting to the office Wi-Fi network. As part of "Questioning the Obvious," which of the following actions should you take first?

  • Reinstall the laptop's network drivers.
  • Replace the office Wi-Fi router with a new one.
  • Verify that the Wi-Fi is enabled on the laptop.
  • Escalate the issue to the network infrastructure team.

Questioning the Obvious: check if Wi-Fi is simply turned off (physical switch or function key). This is the most basic check before any diagnostics, driver reinstalls, hardware replacement, or escalation.

Question 13

Which of the following motherboard form factors BEST allows for low-consumption power supplies?

  • Micro-ATX
  • EATX
  • Mini-ITX
  • NLX

Mini-ITX is a small form factor designed for compact, low-power systems with minimal expansion, making it ideal for low-consumption power supplies. Micro-ATX is mid-size and draws more power. EATX is a large high-performance form factor requiring high-wattage PSUs. NLX is an older slim desktop standard.

Question 14

You are setting up a DSL internet connection for a small office. Which of the following steps should you take to connect the DSL modem to the network?

  • Connect the DSL modem's RJ11 port to the router and the RJ45 port to the phone line.
  • Connect the DSL modem's RJ11 port to the phone line and the RJ45 port to the router.
  • Connect the DSL modem's coaxial port to the phone line and the RJ45 port to the router.
  • Connect the DSL modem's RJ45 port to the phone line and the RJ11 port to the router.

DSL runs over a phone line using an RJ11 connector. The modem's LAN side connects to the router via RJ45 Ethernet. DSL does not use coaxial cable (that's cable internet/DOCSIS). The other options reverse or incorrectly substitute the connector types.

Question 15

A desktop computer powers on (fans spin) but does not boot — screen is blank, no beep codes. The user mentions the system firmware was recently updated. Which action should you take first?

  • Reset the system firmware to its default settings.
  • Remove and re-seat all non-essential adapter cards and cables.
  • Check the power supply unit (PSU) for faults.
  • Replace the CPU with a known good one to check for faults.

The firmware was recently updated — a bad or corrupted update is the most likely cause of a no-POST, no-beep situation. Resetting firmware to defaults directly addresses the recent change. Fans spinning indicate the PSU is delivering power. CPU replacement is a last resort.

Question 16

What is the primary function of an embedded system?

  • To provide a user interface for general computing applications.
  • To manage and control industrial processes and machinery.
  • To operate as a standalone personal computer.
  • To perform a variety of general-purpose computing tasks.

Embedded systems are purpose-built to perform specific dedicated tasks, most classically in industrial control environments. They are not general-purpose computers, do not typically have user interfaces, and are embedded within larger devices rather than operating standalone.

Question 17

Your company upgraded your laptop. You plug your external USB 2.0 hard disk into the new USB 3.0 port. Which of the following is the MOST accurate statement regarding how this device will function?

  • The device will be able to transfer at USB 3.0 speeds.
  • The device will work but only transfer at USB 2.0 speeds.
  • The device will require an external power source.
  • The USB 2.0 device will not work in the USB 3.0 port.

USB 3.0 is backward compatible with USB 2.0 devices. The connection negotiates down to the slower standard (USB 2.0 max 480 Mbps). The device cannot operate at USB 3.0 speeds regardless of port. USB 3.0 ports provide more power than USB 2.0, so no external power source is needed.

Question 18

Which standard is most commonly used for cabled LANs?

  • ISO 9001
  • Wi-Fi Alliance Standard
  • IEEE 802.11
  • IEEE 802.3

IEEE 802.3 is the Ethernet standard governing cabled LANs. ISO 9001 is a quality management standard. Wi-Fi Alliance governs wireless certification. IEEE 802.11 is the wireless LAN (Wi-Fi) standard.

Question 19

Which of the following is a primary function of a mail server?

  • Storing and managing large amounts of structured data
  • Facilitating the sending and receiving of email messages
  • Hosting websites and web applications
  • Providing remote terminal access to network resources

A mail server handles email routing, sending (SMTP), and receiving/storing (POP3/IMAP). Structured data storage describes a database server. Hosting websites describes a web server. Remote terminal access describes an SSH/RDP server.

Question 20

Why are adapter cables important when integrating legacy devices with modern systems?

  • They allow legacy devices to connect to modern systems by converting incompatible interfaces.
  • They are used to upgrade the firmware of legacy devices to be compatible with modern systems.
  • They increase the data transfer rate between legacy and modern devices.
  • They ensure that legacy devices can operate at the same speed as modern devices.

Adapter cables (e.g., DVI-to-HDMI, USB-A-to-USB-C) bridge incompatible physical interfaces, allowing legacy devices to connect to modern systems. They cannot modify firmware, increase speeds beyond the device's native capability, or make legacy hardware perform at modern speeds.
