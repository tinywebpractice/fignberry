10.2.3 Inkjet Printer Imaging Process

Overview

Inkjet printers are suited for low-volume, high-quality color printing such as photos. They are inexpensive to purchase but costly to operate due to expensive ink cartridges and high-grade paper. Compared to laser printers, inkjets are slower and noisier.

Print Head Technologies

Method How It Works Associated Vendors Notes
Thermal Heats ink to form a bubble that bursts, spraying ink onto the paper. HP, Canon, Lexmark Cost-effective but shorter lifespan.
Piezoelectric A piezoelectric element flexes to push ink through the nozzle. Epson Both technologies are licensed to other manufacturers, resulting in re-branded printers.

Carriage System

Images are built line by line as the print head moves back and forth across the paper through a carriage system. Key carriage features include:

  • Unidirectional: Ink is applied only on the outward pass.
  • Bidirectional: Ink is applied on both the outward and return passes, increasing print speed.
  • Platen gap adjustment: Controls the distance between the print head and the paper. It can be set manually or automatically to accommodate thicker media such as cardstock or photo paper.
