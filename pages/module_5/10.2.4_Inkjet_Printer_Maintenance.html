10.2.4 Inkjet Printer Maintenance

General Guidelines

  • Primary maintenance tasks are restocking paper and replacing ink cartridges. Inkjets deplete ink quickly due to high-quality graphics output and lower page yields than laser printers.
  • Do not clean the inside of an inkjet printer; this risks damage. Use a soft, damp cloth on exterior surfaces only.
  • Inkjets have smaller paper trays than laser printers and require more frequent restocking.

Paper Handling and Duplex Assembly

Two common paper path configurations: top-load / bottom-output, or both input and output at the bottom using an "up-and-over" path. Some models include a straight-through rear panel for bulkier media.

Paper path steps:

  1. A load roller moves the top sheet; a separation roller prevents multiple sheets from feeding simultaneously.
  2. A sensor detects the paper once sufficiently advanced. The stepper motor advances the paper as the print head completes each pass.
  3. Eject rollers deliver the paper to the duplexing assembly, if installed and selected, or the output bin.

Inkjets can use standard copier/laser paper, but premium, less absorbent inkjet paper produces better results. Inkjet paper is often single-sided, so ensure correct orientation when loading.

Ink Cartridges

  • Thermal print heads, used by HP, Canon, and Lexmark, are typically built into the cartridge and treated as consumables. Epson piezoelectric print heads are non-removable and designed to last as long as the printer.
  • Cartridge reservoirs have sensors to report ink levels. At minimum, a color printer needs four CMYK reservoirs.
  • Cartridge configurations vary: single combined cartridge, separate black and color cartridges, or individual cartridges per color. Some models add light cyan and light magenta for a wider color gamut.
  • When the driver detects an empty cartridge, it prompts replacement. Follow the printer's instruction manual for the correct procedure.

Other Maintenance Operations

Access these via the Maintenance or Tools tab on the printer's property sheet or control panel:

Operation Symptom / Trigger How to Perform
Print head alignment Output is skewed or vertical lines are misaligned. Run alignment function from the property sheet. Typically done automatically on cartridge replacement.
Print head cleaning Missing lines or gaps in output from a blocked or dirty nozzle. Run the cleaning cycle from the property sheet or control panel. If ineffective, use commercial inkjet cleaning products.
Clearing paper jams Paper misfed or stuck inside the printer. Open the access panel and gently remove jammed paper. Ensure no torn pieces remain. Do not use excessive force.
