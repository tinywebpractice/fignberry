10.2.5 Thermal Printer Maintenance

Overview

Thermal printers use a heating element to create images on paper. The most common type is the direct thermal printer, used for high-volume barcode printing, label printing, and receipts. They are typically portable or small form factor. Resolution is usually 200-300 dpi. Print speed is measured in inches per second (IPS), not pages per minute.

Direct Thermal Imaging Process

Direct thermal printers require special thermal paper containing heat-sensitive chemicals that react and change color when heated by the thermal printhead. No ink, toner, or ribbon is used.

Key components include the thermal printhead, heated pins, thermal paper, and rubber-coated feed roller driven by a stepper motor. Paper and labels can be in fanfold or roll format.

Feed mechanism: a stepper motor turns a rubber-coated roller to friction-feed the paper through the print mechanism.

Maintenance

Task Details
Replacing paper roll Use the correct size and type for your printer model. Open the case, insert the roll with the shiny, heat-sensitive side facing outward, and ensure the paper end is held by the print head when closing.
Cleaning paper debris Tearing receipts across serrated teeth creates paper dust. Remove buildup with a vacuum or soft brush.
Cleaning sticky label residue Labels that separate from the backing can leave adhesive residue. Train users on proper label loading. Clean the print head with isopropyl alcohol (IPA) on a swab, or use cleaning cards.
