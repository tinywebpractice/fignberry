9.3.1 Laptop Disassembly Processes

Overview

Laptops use specialized hardware in a portable chassis and can run on battery or AC power. They use the same operating systems as desktop PCs and, unlike smartphones/tablets, have some upgradeable or replaceable components.

Distinctive built-in features: built-in screen, integrated keyboard, touchpad pointer control, I/O ports on the side of the chassis.

Hand Tools and Parts

  • Laptops use smaller screws than desktops -- use precision screwdrivers; wrong size can strip screws easily.
  • Laptops use different screw sizes throughout; document the size and location of each screw and the location/orientation of ribbon cables and connectors before removal.
  • Tip: photograph the underside of the laptop, print it out, and tape removed screws to their corresponding location on the photo.
  • Photograph each disassembly stage to know where to refit cables and connectors.
  • Organize removed parts away from the main work area to avoid accidental damage.
  • Store static-sensitive components -- SSDs, memory modules, adapter cards -- in antistatic packaging.

Form Factors and Plastics/Frames

  • The laptop chassis incorporates the motherboard, power supply, display screen, keyboard, and touchpad.
  • Plastic or aluminum frames cover internal components; secured with small screws or pressure tabs. Screws may be hidden under rubber or plastic tabs.
  • Obtain the manufacturer's service documentation before any upgrade or replacement work -- it covers chassis disassembly and removal of items like plastic bezels without damage.
  • Only perform disassembly if a warranty option is not available.
