9.3.3 RAM and Adapter Replacement

Overview

Laptops have fewer field-replaceable units (FRUs) than desktops, but designs have become better standardized. Components sourced from the laptop vendor are still recommended. Some FRUs are accessible by removing a screw plate on the underside, typically exposing the fixed disk, optical drive, memory modules, and possibly adapter card slots (Wi-Fi, cellular).

Upgrading RAM Modules

  • Laptop RAM uses SODIMMs (Small Outline Dual In-line Memory Modules).
  • SODIMM slots accept only one specific DDR type. Slots are keyed to prevent incompatible modules from being inserted (e.g., DDR4 SODIMM cannot install in a DDR3 slot).
  • Modules stack one over the other; release side catches and the module pops up at a 45-degree angle for removal.
  • One slot may be panel-accessible; another may require more extensive chassis disassembly.
  • Other form factors include Mini-DIMM and Micro-DIMM, which are smaller than SODIMM and used in some ultraportable models.
  • Always check vendor documentation before obtaining parts.

Upgrading Adapter Cards

  • Adapters for modems, wireless cards, and SSD storage may be accessible via screw-down panels.
  • Common laptop adapter formats include Mini PCIe, mSATA, and M.2. None are compatible with one another.
  • Mini PCIe and M.2 adapters can provide Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and/or cellular data connectivity.
  • When replacing a wireless adapter, reconnect the antenna wires from the old adapter or install a new antenna kit. Antenna wires are typically routed around the screen in the lid, and connections are delicate and fiddly.
  • If installing an adapter with GSM or LTE cellular functionality, insert the SIM card as well.
