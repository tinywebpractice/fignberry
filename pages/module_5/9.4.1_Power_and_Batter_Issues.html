9.4.1 Power and Battery Issues

AC Power Troubleshooting

  • Test the outlet with a known good device (e.g., a lamp) first.
  • Check that the LED on the AC adapter is green. If no LED, check the fuse on the plug; try a known good adapter if available.
  • If an underpowered adapter is used (e.g., a 65 W adapter on a 90 W system), the laptop will display a warning at boot time.

Battery Calibration

  • If the correct AC adapter is confirmed and power consumption seems off, recalibrate the battery.
  • Over time the power meter drifts out of sync with actual remaining charge. Calibration resets the meter to reflect accurate percentages.

Device Will Not Power On (Battery)

  • Check that the battery is seated properly in its compartment.
  • Check for dirty battery contacts; clean with swabs and isopropyl alcohol (90% or higher).
  • If the device does not power on or stays on only a few seconds, the battery is likely fully discharged or at end of life (poor health = will not hold a charge).
  • Test with a known good battery. If a known good battery also fails, the problem is in the power circuitry on the motherboard.
  • Laptop batteries are replaceable. Most smartphones and tablets have non-removable batteries; these must be returned to the vendor for replacement.

Improper Charging

  • Use only the manufacturer-provided charger or an approved replacement. Incorrect cables or extreme heat carry risks of fire or explosion.
  • Do not leave chargers unattended near flammable materials; ensure adequate ventilation.
  • Li-ion batteries should not be fully discharged regularly or kept persistently at 100% charge; both reduce battery life.
  • Short battery life typically means the battery needs replacing. If the battery is not old or faulty, suspect an app causing excessive drain; use a battery utilization app to identify the culprit.
  • Use OS/device power management features (e.g., Battery Saver, Low Power Mode) to prolong battery life.
  • Subscribe to the vendor's alerting service to stay informed of product recalls or safety advisories; manufacturing defects in batteries and AC adapters often occur in batches.

Swollen Battery

  • Signs of a swollen battery: device wobbles when placed flat on a desk; deformed touchpad or keyboard; visible bulge from the battery compartment.
  • Discontinue use of the device immediately if swelling is observed.
  • Swelling indicates a problem with the charging circuit (overcharging protection failure) or liquid damage.
  • Li-ion batteries are designed to swell rather than burst or explode, but a swollen battery is a fire hazard and may leak hazardous chemicals. Avoid skin and eye contact.
  • If the battery cannot be released safely, contact the manufacturer for guidance.
  • Do not discard via standard recycling unless the facility confirms it can accept potentially hazardous batteries. Follow manufacturer disposal instructions.
