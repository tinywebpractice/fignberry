9.4.2 Hardware Failure Issues

Overheating Symptoms

  • Compact design makes mobile devices vulnerable to overheating. Common causes: soft surfaces blocking vents, use on laps, insufficient clearance between vents and walls, dust trapped in vents (acts as insulator).
  • Laptop cooling/chiller pads sit under the laptop to maximize airflow and prevent burns.
  • Handheld devices use passive cooling and can become warm under intensive use. High screen brightness, flashlight use, and direct sunlight accelerate overheating.
  • Devices have protective circuitry that initiates a shutdown at the maximum safe temperature.
  • Use an app (e.g., CPU-Z) to monitor battery temperature. Approaching 40 degrees C is considered too warm.

Liquid Damage Symptoms

  • Waterproofing is rated on the Ingress Protection (IP) scale. Format: two digits (e.g., IP67).
Digit Rates Values
First Solids 5 = dust-protected; 6 = dust-proof; X = unrated
Second Liquids 7 = immersion up to 1 m; 8 = immersion beyond 1 m
  • If dust protection is unrated, the rating will appear as IPX7 or IPX8.
  • Signs of liquid damage: visible water under the screen, graphics artifacts, no image displayed.
  • Even without visible signs, power off the device immediately if liquid damage is suspected. Dry excess liquid as much as possible.
  • If internal components may have been exposed, disassemble to fully dry, then clean circuit boards and contacts. The battery will usually need to be replaced.

Physically Damaged Port Symptoms

  • Improper insertion/removal of connectors can damage external ports. Symptoms: loose connector, connector no longer fits, no or intermittent data connection, failure to charge.
  • Educate users to remove connectors by gripping the connector body and pulling straight; never jiggle to remove.
  • USB-C and Lightning connectors are reversible. Other connector types must be oriented correctly before insertion.
