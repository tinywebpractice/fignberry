9.4.4 Screen and Calibration Issues

Display Troubleshooting Overview

  • Isolate the problem to a specific component: graphics adapter, display panel, backlight, or digitizer.
  • No image on screen: test the GPU by connecting an external monitor. If the GPU is good, the backlight may have failed (a dim image will still be faintly visible on the panel).
  • Most screens use LED backlights. Older laptops may use an inverter to power a fluorescent backlight.
  • Cracked or broken plastics around the case are mostly cosmetic unless a severe break exposes internal components. Worn hinges that no longer hold up the screen must be replaced.

Broken Screen Issues

  • Impacts on hard surfaces from over 1 m typically crack or shatter the glass. If only the glass layer is damaged, the digitizer and display may remain partially usable.
  • A broken screen usually requires warranty or professional repair.
  • No visible cracks but screen/touch issues: suspect liquid damage to the screen or digitizer circuitry.

Digitizer Issues

Symptoms: touchscreen not responding to input, stylus not working. After ruling out shock and liquid damage, try these steps:

  • Verify the touchscreen and the user's fingers are clean and dry.
  • If a screen protector is fitted, confirm it is fully adhered with no bubbles or lifted edges.
  • Restart the device to rule out a transitory software problem.
  • Try the device in a different location; electromagnetic interference (EMI) can affect digitizer operation.
  • If the device was recently serviced, verify all digitizer wires are correctly reconnected ("What has changed?").

Cursor Drift / Touch Calibration Issues

  • Touchpad sensitivity too high can cause typing vibrations to move the cursor (pointer drift, ghost cursor while typing). Install up-to-date drivers and adjust input sensitivity settings. Many laptops have an Fn key to disable the touchpad.
  • Unresponsive or inaccurate touch input (after ruling out hardware) may indicate insufficient resources (too many open apps) or a poorly written app hogging memory.
  • A soft reset usually resolves the problem short-term. If persistent, identify whether a specific app is the cause or free space by removing data/apps.
  • Windows and some Android versions support touch recalibration utilities. If no cause is identified, warranty repair is likely needed.
