9.6.6 Exercise: Peripheral Design Challenge Capstone

About This Exercise

This is a CompTIA A+ Core 1 (220-1201) creative design capstone activity supporting exam objective 1.3: Given a scenario, configure basic mobile device network connectivity and provide application support. The goal is to design an innovative peripheral device for a specific user group, explain how it functions and integrates with existing systems, and present it clearly.

Requirements: research existing peripherals, identify a user need, design a device with labeled components, write a 2 to 3 paragraph paper, and prepare a 3 to 5 minute presentation.

Device Concept: FocusDock

A compact, one-cable USB-C docking peripheral designed for remote workers and students who move between locations throughout the day. Most people carry a laptop but stop short of bringing a full hub because typical hubs are large, have dangling cables, and require setup at each location. FocusDock solves this by combining a folding USB-C hub, a 60W pass-through charger, a physical webcam privacy shutter panel, and a silent ambient sound module into a single palm-sized unit that snaps flat against the back of a laptop lid via a magnetic mount.

Specification Detail
Target user Remote workers, students, mobile professionals
Primary function Single-cable instant workstation expansion
Connection USB-C (Thunderbolt 4 compatible); 60W pass-through charging
Ports provided USB-A x2, HDMI 2.0, USB-C data, 3.5mm audio
Additional features Physical webcam shutter panel, ambient sound module (white noise), magnetic laptop mount
OS compatibility Windows 10/11, macOS 12+, ChromeOS, Linux (plug-and-play, no drivers required)
Form factor Folds flat; 110 x 55 x 12 mm when closed
Power source Bus-powered via USB-C; pass-through for laptop charging

Device Diagram

Paper

FocusDock addresses a gap that affects remote workers and students daily: the friction between mobility and productivity. Existing hubs require unpacking, plugging in multiple cables, and leaving a bundle of hardware on the desk when you move. FocusDock eliminates that overhead. One USB-C cable connects the device to any laptop, instantly providing two USB-A ports, HDMI output, audio, and 60W pass-through charging. The physical webcam shutter panel slides over the laptop camera without requiring any software, and the embedded ambient sound module operates independently from the host OS via a dedicated audio channel. The magnetic mount lets the unit travel attached to the laptop lid, always ready, never lost at the bottom of a bag.

FocusDock integrates with existing systems as a plug-and-play USB-C hub requiring no drivers on Windows 10/11, macOS 12+, ChromeOS, or Linux. Thunderbolt 4 compatibility means it also supports DisplayPort alternate mode for HDMI output on systems that support it. The 60W pass-through charging negotiates via USB Power Delivery, meaning the same adapter that powers the hub also charges the laptop. There are no proprietary protocols, no companion apps, and no firmware to update. Any device with a USB-C port supporting data transfer can use FocusDock, which also makes it compatible with tablets and smartphones for charging and audio output.

The user experience improvement over existing hubs is primarily physical and behavioral. Because FocusDock mounts to the back of the laptop lid magnetically, it is always with the device and requires no conscious packing. The folding form factor means the port side folds inward during transport, protecting the ports. The webcam shutter is a deliberate response to the privacy anxiety that many remote workers feel during video calls. Rather than relying on a software toggle that can be overridden, the shutter is a physical barrier that requires no trust in any application. Taken together, these features reduce the number of decisions and setup steps a user must make each time they sit down to work, which is the defining characteristic of a well-designed peripheral.

Presentation Outline (3 to 5 minutes)

  • Opening (30 sec): The problem - remote workers pack and unpack a hub every day, and it still never has the right ports. FocusDock is the hub that stays with the laptop.
  • Who it is for (30 sec): Students, remote workers, mobile professionals - anyone who works from multiple locations and needs a consistent port selection without carrying extra gear.
  • What it does (1 min): Walk through the port array, pass-through charging, webcam shutter, ambient sound module, and magnetic mount. Reference the diagram.
  • How it integrates (1 min): Plug-and-play on all major OS platforms, Thunderbolt 4 compatible, USB Power Delivery for charging. No drivers, no apps, no configuration.
  • What makes it different (1 min): The magnetic mount and folding form factor remove the packing step entirely. The physical webcam shutter is a hardware privacy solution. The ambient sound module requires no OS interaction.
  • Close (30 sec): FocusDock is not a new category of peripheral - it is a better version of one that already exists, designed around how mobile users actually behave rather than how they are assumed to behave.
