11.1.16 Lesson Review

Question 1

Which of the following are true about a policy? (Select two.)

  • Defines who should complete the work.
  • Defines a desired outcome and outlines how that outcome should be met.
  • Identifies the problem, such as why action is needed and which action should be taken.
  • Explains which action should be taken.
  • Includes rules that have been set by an organization to address a particular problem or concern.
  • Defines when the work should be completed.

A policy is an overall statement of intent - it identifies a problem or concern and states the organizational position or required action in response, including the rules established to address it. Defining who completes the work, outlining how outcomes are met, and scheduling are SOP-level details, not policy.

Question 2

Where could a company employee find out how to reset a password or change their voicemail message?

  • Security Policy
  • Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
  • Knowledge base
  • Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) Policy

A knowledge base is a self-serve repository of FAQs and step-by-step troubleshooting procedures. Password resets and voicemail configuration are common end-user tasks documented in knowledge base articles for self-service reference. Policies state intent and rules but do not contain procedural how-to guides.

Question 3

What is the primary purpose of a knowledge base in a support environment?

  • To categorize and prioritize support tickets based on severity and impact.
  • To escalate unresolved tickets to senior technicians or third-party support groups.
  • To track the ownership and status of support tickets during the troubleshooting process.
  • To provide a collection of frequently asked questions (FAQs) and troubleshooting procedures for users.

A knowledge base is a self-serve central repository housing FAQs, how-to articles, and common troubleshooting procedures. Categorization, escalation, and ownership/status tracking are all functions of the ticketing system, not the knowledge base.

Question 4

Why is clear written communication important in the ticket management process?

  • It helps track the ownership and progress of the ticket through different escalation levels.
  • It ensures that tickets are categorized correctly at the time of creation.
  • It allows customers to resolve incidents without contacting the help desk.
  • It ensures that all tickets are escalated to senior technicians for resolution.

At any point in the ticket life cycle, other agents, technicians, or managers may need to make decisions or continue troubleshooting using only the ticket record. Clear progress notes and descriptions ensure anyone picking up the ticket can follow what has been done and what remains. Tickets may also be forwarded to customers as a record of work performed.

Question 5

A company is experiencing a high volume of support tickets for common, repetitive issues. How effective would implementing a Knowledge Base be in addressing this problem?

  • Somewhat effective, as it reduces the workload for Tier 3 support but not for Tier 1 or Tier 2 support
  • Highly effective, as it provides users with self-service options to resolve common issues without filing tickets
  • Ineffective, as it does not address the root cause of the repetitive issues
  • Ineffective, as it primarily focuses on categorizing and prioritizing tickets rather than resolving them

A knowledge base operates at Tier 0, enabling users to resolve common repetitive issues themselves without filing tickets. This directly reduces ticket volume across all tiers. A knowledge base does not need to eliminate root causes to be effective - it reduces support burden by enabling self-service resolution. Categorization and prioritization are ticketing system functions, not knowledge base functions.

Question 6

A company's help desk receives multiple tickets reporting that customers cannot access the company's online payment system. Upon investigation, it is discovered that the issue is affecting all customers and may involve a potential data breach. Based on the standard severity levels, how should this incident be classified?

  • Major Incident
  • Minor Incident
  • Critical Incident
  • Request

A Critical incident has a widespread effect on customers or involves a potential or actual data breach. This scenario meets both criteria. A Major incident affects a limited group or involves a suspected security violation. A Minor incident has no significant effect on customer groups. A Request is a ticket for provisioning items covered by an SOP.

Question 7

Why is categorizing tickets in a ticketing system important?

  • It allows end-users to resolve their own issues without contacting the help desk.
  • It ensures tickets are assigned to the appropriate support section or technician.
  • It guarantees that all tickets are resolved within a specific time frame.
  • It eliminates the need for escalation to senior technicians.

Categorization groups related tickets together to route them to the correct team or technician and to support reporting and analysis. Self-service resolution is the purpose of a knowledge base. Resolution timeframes are governed by SLAs. Categorization does not eliminate escalation - escalation is still required when an agent cannot resolve a ticket.

Question 8

Jason is a sales rep with a computer company that sells customized laptops to the public. He is opening a ticket to help resolve a customer issue with a malfunctioning laptop, which was purchased by an important customer who is relying on the laptop to complete a critical project. Which of the following items in the ticket can Jason use to help the support team immediately determine the time sensitivity of resolving this issue?

  • Tier assignment
  • Detailed issue description
  • Department assignment
  • Severity level

Severity level is the ticket field specifically designed to communicate urgency and impact at a glance. Setting the severity level appropriately immediately signals time sensitivity to the support team without requiring them to read through the full description. Severity levels can also trigger automatic notifications to senior technicians and managers. Tier and department assignment address routing, not urgency.

Question 9

What is the primary purpose of documenting "Lessons Learned" in IT support processes?

  • To track the number of incidents handled by each technician.
  • To identify and assign blame for incidents.
  • To ensure all tickets are closed on time.
  • To improve future processes and prevent recurring issues.

A lessons learned (after-action) report identifies root causes and recommends remediation steps or preventive measures to reduce the risk of recurrence. Outputs include new or revised SOPs and knowledge base articles. It is a forward-looking improvement process, not a blame-assignment or tracking exercise.

Question 10

You are a manager at an IT company, and your team is reviewing the organization's policy documentation to ensure compliance with company standards. During the review, you notice that a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for onboarding new employees includes steps for granting access to IT systems but does not reference the Acceptable Use Policy (AUP). How should you address this issue?

  • Leave the SOP as it is since policies and SOPs are independent of each other and do not need to reference one another.
  • Update the SOP to include a step requiring new employees to review and acknowledge the Acceptable Use Policy (AUP).
  • Create a new policy specifically for onboarding employees to address the gap in the SOP.
  • Remove the SOP entirely, as it does not align with the organization's policies.

SOPs exist to implement policy - they are the step-by-step procedures that ensure policy compliance. Onboarding is the appropriate point to ensure new employees review and acknowledge the AUP before being granted system access. The AUP already exists; a new policy is unnecessary. Removing the SOP would eliminate the structured onboarding process entirely.

Question 11

A company has an SLA with a cloud service provider that guarantees 99.99% uptime for their services. During the past year, the company experienced a total of 2 hours of downtime. Based on the SLA, what should the company do next?

  • File a complaint with the service provider for failing to meet the SLA and request compensation.
  • Escalate the issue to Tier 3 support for further investigation.
  • Terminate the SLA immediately due to the service provider's failure to meet expectations.
  • Review the SLA to determine whether or not 2 hours of downtime is within the acceptable limit for 99.99% uptime.

The correct first step is to review the SLA to confirm whether a breach occurred. The Rule of 4 Nines (99.99%) allows a maximum of approximately 52 minutes of downtime per year - 2 hours exceeds that limit. Only after confirming the breach through the SLA review should the company proceed with the defined recourse process, which may include filing a complaint and seeking compensation.

Question 12

Why is clear written communication important during the ticket management process?

  • It guarantees that all tickets are resolved within the SLA timeframe.
  • It eliminates the need for escalation to higher support tiers.
  • It ensures that all team members understand the ticket's history and current status.
  • It allows end-users to resolve their own issues without contacting support.

Clear and concise progress notes, issue descriptions, and resolution documentation ensure that any agent, technician, or manager picking up the ticket at any stage can immediately understand what has happened and what remains to be done. Clear writing does not guarantee SLA compliance, eliminate escalation, or enable end-user self-service.

Question 13

A help desk team is receiving multiple tickets from users reporting that they cannot access a shared drive. Upon reviewing the tickets, you notice that all the affected users are from the same department, and the issue started after a recent server update. What should you do to address the situation effectively?

  • Escalate one ticket to Tier 2 and close the rest, assuming they are duplicates.
  • Reassign the tickets to different agents to speed up resolution.
  • Treat each ticket as a separate incident and troubleshoot them individually.
  • Analyze the tickets to identify a common cause and open a problem ticket for further investigation.

When the help desk receives multiple incidents of the same type, this signals a problem requiring a problem ticket. A problem ticket represents the root cause requiring analysis and service reconfiguration. The server update is the probable cause. Closing tickets assumes duplication without confirming it. Distributing or treating tickets individually wastes effort and ignores the clear pattern pointing to a shared root cause.

Question 14

Which of the following is the correct definition of a "Critical Incident"?

  • An incident that affects a limited group of customers or involves a suspected security violation.
  • An incident that is not having a significant effect on customer groups.
  • An incident that has a widespread effect on customers or involves a potential or actual data breach.
  • An incident related to provisioning things that the IT department has a SOP for, such as setting up new user accounts.

A Critical incident has a widespread effect on customers or involves a potential or actual data breach - either condition alone qualifies it as critical. A limited group affected with a suspected violation is Major. No significant customer effect is Minor. Provisioning items covered by an SOP describes a Request, not an incident.

Question 15

A security manager wants to create a step-by-step list of the completed actions for any given task to comply with policy. What is the manager creating?

  • Elasticity
  • Scalability
  • SOP
  • Confidentiality

A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is defined as a step-by-step list of actions that must be completed for a given task to comply with policy. Elasticity and scalability are infrastructure/cloud concepts. Confidentiality is a security principle (CIA triad), not a document type.
