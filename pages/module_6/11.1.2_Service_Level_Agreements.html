11.1.2 Service Level Agreements

Service Level Agreements (SLAs)

An SLA defines the level of service requirements between a provider and a customer, either internally (between departments) or externally (with a third-party vendor).

Internal SLA examples
Access to shared hardware resources between departments; a maintenance department detailing how building support is provided to other departments.
External SLA examples
ISP agreements specifying throughput metrics for an internet connection; cloud service provider agreements detailing delivery metrics for cloud resources.

SLA Contents

  • Description of the service being provided
  • Metrics used to measure the level of service
  • Expected uptime and availability windows
  • Definition of what does and does not constitute downtime
  • Recourse process for filing a complaint if metrics are not met
  • Financial recovery amounts available if the SLA is breached

Rule of Nines

The Rule of Nines is a common metric for expressing uptime availability and data durability in SLAs.

Rule Availability Max Downtime / Impact Use
Rule of 4 Nines 99.99% Max 52 minutes downtime per year Uptime / availability
Rule of 11 Nines 99.999999999% Max 315.58 microseconds downtime per year Uptime / availability
Rule of 11 Nines (durability) 99.999999999% With 1 billion objects in storage, zero objects lost over 100 years Data/file storage durability
