11.1.4 Categories and Severity

Ticket Types (Categories)

Categories and subcategories group related tickets for routing, reporting, and analysis. Service management standards define three basic ticket types:

Requests
Tickets for provisioning items covered by an existing SOP: new user accounts, hardware/software purchases, server deployments, etc. Complex requests not covered by existing procedures are better handled as projects.
Incidents
Tickets related to errors or unexpected situations faced by end-users or customers. Further categorized by severity: minor, major, and critical.
Problems
Tickets representing the root cause of one or more incidents, requiring analysis and service reconfiguration to resolve. Typically generated internally when the help desk receives many incidents of the same type.

Practical Category Strategies

End-users cannot reliably distinguish incidents from problems, so exposing raw ticket types to them is not practical. Two common strategies:

  • Simplified top-level categories for end-users: A short list of self-service options such as New Device Request, New App Request, Employee Onboarding, Employee Offboarding, Help/Support, and Security Incident. Technicians then apply a longer internal subcategory list for reporting and analysis.
  • Keyword tagging: Standard keyword tags added to each ticket. More flexible than fixed categories but depends on consistent tagging by technicians.

Severity Levels

Severity classifies tickets into priority order. A three-level system based on impact is commonly sufficient:

Severity Definition
Critical Widespread effect on customers, or involves a potential or actual data breach
Major Affects a limited group of customers, or involves a suspected security violation
Minor No significant effect on customer groups

High-volume environments may require more discrete levels measuring both impact and urgency. Severity levels can also trigger automatic notifications to senior technicians and managers for major and critical incidents.
