Professional Support Process Overview

From first contact, the support process must reassure customers that their inquiry will be handled efficiently. A faulty support experience compounds a customer's negative impression of the company. Support contact information and hours of operation should be well-advertised, and documentation should set clear expectations around supported items, resolution timelines, and repair vs. replace policies.

Set Expectations and Timeline

On receiving a request, acknowledge it and set expectations immediately. Repeat the request back to the customer, state the next steps, and establish a timeline. Clarify the following:

  • The customer's expectations of what will be done and when
  • The customer's concerns about cost or business impact
  • Your constraints - time, parts, costs, and contractual obligations

Meet Expectations and Timeline

  • Resolve in one call if possible; escalate promptly if not
  • Drive problem acceptance and resolution - either work on it yourself or ensure the assigned person/department has accepted it
  • Monitor open tickets and re-prioritize to prevent SLA failures
  • Proactively update the customer - they should never need to call to find out what is happening
  • If the company has let the customer down, be accurate and honest; do not make excuses. Empathize and commit to a positive action with a specific timeline

Repair and Replace Options

Repair
Provide clear instructions for packing and returning the item to a repair center. Issue a ticket-tracking number and returned-merchandise authorization (RMA). Keep the customer updated on repair progress.
Replace
Provide clear instructions for product delivery or reordering, and clarify whether the defective product must be returned.

Follow Up and Ticket Closure

  • After resolution, give the customer a general explanation of what caused the issue and what was done to fix it, with assurance it is unlikely to recur
  • Thank the customer for their time and assistance upon closing the call
  • Arrange a follow-up call if appropriate to verify the issue has not recurred and the customer is satisfied
  • Log the ticket as closed once the solution has been tested, verified, and the customer has expressed satisfaction; send verification via email or phone call

Communication Best Practices (Video)

  • Avoid jargon: Customers do not share technical lingo. Acronyms like ACL, VPN, FAT32, VoIP, and 2FA are meaningless to most end users. Use plain language appropriate to the individual.
  • Cultural sensitivity: Help desks increasingly support global customers. Words and phrases carry different meanings across cultures and regions. Be mindful of who you are speaking with and adjust accordingly.
  • Handling upset customers: Be upfront about the problem, explain it simply, set proper expectations, and communicate clearly. Follow through by educating the end user so they understand what happened and can avoid recreating the issue.
  • Close the loop: Do not just fix the problem and move on. Explain the situation, confirm the resolution, and ensure the customer is positioned to be successful going forward.
