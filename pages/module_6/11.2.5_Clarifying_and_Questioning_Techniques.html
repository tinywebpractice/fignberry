11.2.5 Clarifying and Questioning Techniques

Question Types

Type Definition Example
Open-ended Invites the customer to compose a free-form response; surfaces the customer's perception of the problem "What seems to be the problem?"
Closed Can only be answered with Yes/No or a single fixed response; used to confirm or rule out specific symptoms "What error number is displayed on the panel?"

Questioning Strategy

  • Start with open-ended questions to let the customer describe the problem in their own words.
  • Guide without leading - avoid phrasing that causes the customer to simply confirm your guess.
  • As a picture of the problem emerges, switch to pertinent closed questions to prove or disprove your perception.
  • When a customer is rambling or not reaching the point, take charge by restating the issue and asking targeted closed questions.
  • Do not fire off a rapid sequence of closed questions - this can overwhelm and confuse the customer.
  • Establish the customer's technical level early and target the conversation accordingly.

Clarifying Vague or Ambiguous Statements

When a customer's information is unclear, ask specific follow-up questions to pin down meaning. Examples:

  • "What did the error message say?"
  • "When you say the printout is dark, is there a faint image or is it completely black?"
  • "Is the power LED on the printer lit?"

Example: Open vs. Closed in Practice

Approach Agent Response Outcome
Open question (less effective here) "What does the screen look like?" Customer describes their perception ("It's dark") without providing useful diagnostic data
Restate + closed question (more effective) "Pressing a key should activate the monitor, but since that isn't happening, can you tell me whether the light on the monitor is green?" Customer reports a specific symptom ("There's a yellow light") that advances diagnosis
