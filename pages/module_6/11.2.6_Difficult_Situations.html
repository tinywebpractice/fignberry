11.2.6 Difficult Situations

Overview

A difficult situation occurs when either party becomes, or risks becoming, angry or upset. The goal is to defuse tension using deliberate techniques. Think of the situation as difficult - not the customer. Do not personalize support issues.

Maintain a Positive Attitude

An angry customer is usually frustrated that something is not working or feels let down. Empathizing with the customer builds a positive relationship and signals that you want to resolve the problem. Apologizing does not mean agreeing - it means acknowledging their point of view.

Example: "I'm sorry you're having a problem with your new PC. Let's see what we can do to sort things out..."

Avoid the following behaviors:

  • Arguing with the customer - Remain calm; only advance facts and practical suggestions that move the case toward resolution.
  • Denying a problem exists or dismissing its importance - If the customer has escalated to complaining, the issue is clearly important to them. Acknowledge it and demonstrate how it can be resolved, regardless of whether you consider it trivial.
  • Being judgmental - Do not assume the customer lacks knowledge about the system and is therefore causing the problem.
