11.4.2 Documentation Policies and Procedures

Why Documentation Matters

Without documented policies, procedures, and regulations, chaotic situations cause people to act outside their authority and make poor decisions. Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) provide a plan of action so that when things go wrong, there is a defined, reliable response rather than improvisation.

Regulations, Policies, and Procedures

Type Source Description Example
Regulation Government agency (federal, state, or local) Mandated rules that apply uniformly across organizations to ensure safety and compliance Building codes requiring evacuation maps near elevators; chemical labeling requirements in the workplace
Policy Government regulation or internal company decision Rules established by an organization; may be required by regulation or created independently by company administrators; must be documented so staff can reference them Regulation-based: hiring policies for people with disabilities. Internal: dress code, ESD wrist strap requirements in certain work areas
Procedure Internal (created by the organization) Step-by-step instructions for completing a specific task; critical for high-risk or complex operations where an incorrect step can cause serious damage or safety hazards Documented steps for transitioning high-powered battery backup systems; following each step in sequence to avoid reversing electrical polarity

Key Distinctions

  • Regulations are almost always externally imposed by a government body.
  • Policies may derive from regulations or be created entirely internally; they govern how the organization operates day-to-day.
  • Procedures are operational step-by-step guides; they eliminate ambiguity and prevent errors during execution of critical tasks.
