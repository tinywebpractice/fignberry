11.4.4 Exercise: Elevator Pitch Practice

About This Exercise

This is a CompTIA hands-on activity supporting A+ Core 2 exam objective 4.7 (professional communication techniques). The goal is to craft and deliver a concise, professional elevator pitch of no more than 30 seconds, submitted as a video or audio recording.

Elevator Pitch Structure

Element Purpose Example
Who you are Introduce yourself by name and briefly describe your role or background "Hi, my name is [Name], and I'm a [Role/Title]."
What you do Highlight key skills, areas of expertise, or the project you are working on "I specialize in [Expertise/Skills] and am currently working on [Project/Goal]."
Why it matters Explain the value you bring or the significance of your project "This project is focused on [Key Impact/Value]."
Call to action (optional) Conclude with a statement that encourages further conversation or collaboration "I'm excited about [Future Goals] and would love to discuss how we can collaborate."

Delivery Checklist

  • Pitch stays within the 30-second time limit
  • Speak clearly and with enthusiasm
  • Maintain a positive, polished, and professional tone
  • Content is clear and easy to understand
  • Effectively communicates your value or the importance of your project
  • Sounds natural and confident

Sample Pitch (IT Support Context)

"Hi, my name is [Name], and I'm an IT support technician currently studying for my CompTIA A+ certification. I specialize in hardware troubleshooting, OS support, and end-user communication across Windows and Linux environments. My goal is to help organizations reduce downtime by resolving technical issues efficiently and professionally. I'd love to connect with anyone working in IT support or helpdesk roles to learn more about opportunities in the field."

Reflection Prompts

  • What did you find most challenging about creating your elevator pitch?
  • How did practicing your pitch improve your delivery and confidence?
  • How can you use this skill in future professional interactions such as interviews, networking events, or client introductions?
