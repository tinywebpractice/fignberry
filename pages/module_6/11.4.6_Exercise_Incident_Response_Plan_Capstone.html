11.4.6 Exercise: Incident Response Plan Capstone

About This Exercise

This is a CompTIA capstone activity supporting A+ Core 2 exam objectives 4.1 and 4.7. The scenario: you are a cybersecurity analyst for a mid-sized company tasked with drafting a basic Incident Response Plan (IRP) to guide the team when handling security incidents. The plan must cover four components: Incident Reporting, Incident Documentation, Incident Resolution, and a Communication Plan.

Incident Response Plan (IRP)

1. Incident Reporting

Any employee who identifies a potential security incident must report it immediately using the company ticketing system. If the ticketing system is unavailable, the incident should be reported by phone or email to the IT Security team. All incidents must be reported within 30 minutes of identification.

Responsible parties: end users report initial observations; IT Security team owns the ticket from creation onward; supervisors are notified if the incident affects business operations.

Sample incident reporting form:

Field Example / Instructions
Reporter name Full name of the person submitting the report
Department Reporter's department or team
Date and time reported MM/DD/YYYY HH:MM
Date and time incident occurred (if known) MM/DD/YYYY HH:MM
Incident description Brief description of what was observed (e.g., "Suspicious email received with attachment; user clicked link")
Systems or assets affected Hostnames, IP addresses, applications, or data involved
Any actions already taken Steps the reporter took before submitting (e.g., "Disconnected from network")
Reporting method Ticketing system / phone / email

2. Incident Documentation

All incidents must be documented in the ticketing system immediately upon report. Accurate and complete records are critical for analysis, regulatory compliance, and post-incident review. Tickets must never be deleted; they must be updated throughout the lifecycle of the incident.

Required fields for every ticket:

Field Description
Ticket ID Auto-generated unique identifier
Date and time of incident When the incident occurred or was first detected
Date and time reported When the ticket was created
Incident category e.g., malware, phishing, unauthorized access, data loss
Priority level Critical / High / Medium / Low (assigned by IT Security)
Description Detailed account of what occurred
Systems or data affected Specific assets, accounts, or data sets involved
Actions taken Chronological log of all mitigation and response steps, with timestamps and responsible party for each entry
Current status Open / In Progress / Escalated / Resolved / Closed
Assigned technician Name of the IT staff member handling the ticket

3. Incident Resolution

Resolution follows a structured sequence to ensure consistent, traceable handling of every incident:

Step Action
1. Initial assessment IT Security reviews the ticket, confirms the incident is valid, and determines scope and impact
2. Prioritization Assign priority level (Critical / High / Medium / Low) based on business impact and urgency; Critical incidents receive immediate response
3. Assignment Ticket is assigned to the appropriate technician or team; escalate to senior staff or external specialists if the issue exceeds current capability
4. Containment Take immediate steps to prevent the incident from spreading (e.g., isolate affected systems, revoke compromised credentials)
5. Resolution Implement a fix or workaround; document every action taken with timestamps in the ticket
6. Verification Confirm the resolution is effective; verify with the affected user or system that normal operation has been restored
7. Ticket closure Update the ticket status to Closed; record the final resolution summary and time to resolve
8. Post-incident review Within 5 business days of closure, conduct a review to identify root cause, evaluate the response, and document lessons learned and any recommended policy or procedure changes

4. Communication Plan

Clear communication throughout an incident prevents misinformation, ensures appropriate parties can act, and maintains stakeholder confidence.

Stakeholder When to Notify Method
IT Security team Immediately upon ticket creation Ticketing system alert + direct message
IT Manager All High and Critical incidents; within 1 hour of detection Phone call + email
Affected department supervisor When business operations are impacted Email or phone
Executive leadership Critical incidents or confirmed data breaches Direct briefing by IT Manager
Affected end users When their systems or data are involved; at resolution Email or in-person
Legal / Compliance If regulated data (PII, financial, health records) is involved Email + formal incident report
External parties (customers, vendors) Only if their data or services are affected; coordinated by leadership and legal Formal written notification per legal guidance

Status updates must be posted to the ticket at a minimum of every 2 hours during active High/Critical incidents. All external communications must be reviewed by IT management and legal before release.
