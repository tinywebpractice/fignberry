11.5 Module Quiz

Question 1

A helpdesk operator schedules a follow-up call with a user, but the ticket queue gets swamped, and they realize they will not be able to make the call on time. What should the operator do?

  • Send a replacement.
  • Demonstrate respect for the customer's property.
  • Avoid distractions.
  • Communicate with the end-user.

Per 11.2.2, if it becomes obvious you will not be on time for a scheduled contact, you must inform the customer as soon as possible. The professional response to a missed appointment is proactive communication, not silence. The other options are valid professionalism principles but do not apply to this specific scenario.

Question 2

Which of the following statements is true about macOS?

  • macOS is available in editions for both business workstations and home PCs.
  • macOS can be installed on both Apple and non-Apple PCs.
  • macOS is derived from the UNIX kernel.
  • macOS supports touch screens on all Apple devices.

macOS is derived from a UNIX kernel with additional code for its graphical interface and system utilities (11.3.1). Separate business/home editions describe Windows, not macOS. macOS is exclusive to Apple hardware. macOS supports the Magic Trackpad but not touch screens - touch is an iOS/iPadOS feature.

Question 3

Where could a company employee find out how to reset a password or change their voicemail message?

  • Security Policy
  • Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
  • Knowledge base
  • Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) Policy

A knowledge base is a self-service repository of FAQs, how-to guides, and step-by-step instructions for common tasks. A Security Policy defines rules for securing systems. An AUP governs acceptable behavior with company technology. A BYOD Policy governs personal device use - none of these provide procedural how-to guidance for end users.

Question 4

A Tier 1 support agent is documenting a ticket for an issue they could not resolve and is escalating it to Tier 2. The agent writes the following note: "User reports an application crash. Restarted the application, but the issue persists. No further troubleshooting performed." What is the primary issue with this documentation, and how could it impact the ticket management process?

  • The note lacks sufficient detail about the troubleshooting steps performed, which could delay resolution.
  • The note does not include the user's contact information, which violates documentation standards.
  • The note does not indicate whether the user was informed of the escalation, which could cause confusion for the user.
  • The note fails to categorize the issue as critical, which could lead to improper prioritization.

Good ticket documentation requires a complete record of all actions taken so the next technician can continue without duplicating work. "No further troubleshooting performed" tells Tier 2 nothing about what was investigated or ruled out. Contact information is captured in the ticket's requester field. User notification of escalation is a communication concern, not a documentation deficiency. No indication in the scenario suggests critical classification is warranted.

Question 5

As you arrive on-site to fix an issue, you are met by a frantic employee who immediately details how critical the issue is and how important it is to fix it as quickly as possible. Having seen this same issue many times, you say to the customer, "Stop worrying. I know what I'm doing." Which of the following professionalism principles are you failing to follow?

  • Avoid being judgmental.
  • Maintain a positive attitude.
  • Avoid an argument with the customer.
  • Avoid dismissing customer problems.

"Stop worrying" dismisses the customer's concern. Per 11.2.6, you must not deny that a problem exists or dismiss its importance - if the customer considers it urgent, that must be acknowledged regardless of how routine it seems. No judgment about the customer's knowledge is occurring, no argument is taking place, and projecting confidence is part of a positive attitude. The specific failure is dismissiveness.

Question 6

Which of the following is the correct definition of a "Critical Incident"?

  • An incident that affects a limited group of customers or involves a suspected security violation.
  • An incident that is not having a significant effect on customer groups.
  • An incident that has a widespread effect on customers or involves a potential or actual data breach.
  • An incident related to provisioning things that the IT department has a SOP for, such as setting up new user accounts.

A Critical Incident is defined by widespread customer impact or involvement of a potential or actual data breach - both representing the highest priority tier requiring immediate escalation. A limited scope or suspected (unconfirmed) violation describes a lower priority. Low impact describes a low-priority incident. Routine provisioning tasks with an established SOP are standard service requests, not incidents.

Question 7

A Help Desk technician receives a call from a user who is unable to download new messages from the company's email server. The technician tells the user to open a command prompt, ping the email server, and check the SMTP and POP3 server IP addresses. Which of the following mistakes did the technician make?

  • The technician did not have the user check the workstation's IP address first.
  • The technician had a negative attitude and failed to take notes.
  • The technician did not walk to the user's desk and conduct the tests for the user.
  • The technician used jargon that the user probably didn't understand.

Terms like "ping," "SMTP," and "POP3" are technical jargon a typical user may not understand, violating the plain-language communication principle from 11.2.3. There is no documented requirement to check the workstation IP first, no indication of a negative attitude or missing notes, and remote guidance via phone is an acceptable support method.

Question 8

A phone operator at a helpdesk is working with several frustrated customers and is put in various difficult positions. Which of the following is NOT part of handling difficult situations properly?

  • Stay calm.
  • Post on social media.
  • Actively listen.
  • Have a positive attitude.

Posting about customer experiences on social media is explicitly prohibited under any circumstances (11.2.8). Stay calm, actively listen, and maintain a positive attitude are all correct techniques for handling difficult situations (11.2.6, 11.2.7).

Question 9

You are consulting for a small business that needs to choose an operating system for its new office setup. The business requires an OS that can integrate seamlessly into a centrally managed business domain network, supports a wide range of hardware options, and allows for flexibility in choosing devices from multiple vendors. After analyzing the requirements, which operating system would you recommend?

  • Linux (Fedora)
  • macOS
  • Windows 11
  • Chrome OS

Windows 11 is the business client OS designed for centrally managed domain networks, runs on hardware from any vendor, and provides the broadest enterprise domain integration (11.3.1). macOS is limited to Apple hardware, eliminating the multi-vendor requirement. Fedora lacks native Windows domain integration. Chrome OS targets budget/education markets on specific hardware, not enterprise domain environments.

Question 10

The lead for a company's vulnerability management program is looking at the mobile aspect of the company's program. They started with end of life devices, then addressed patching statuses, and are now looking at hardening. Which of the following has the widest variety of change and will prove the most difficult?

  • Android
  • iPadOS
  • iOS
  • Windows

Android's open-source nature and manufacturer-controlled update deployment creates significant fragmentation across OS versions, patch levels, and hardware configurations, making hardening the most complex of any mobile platform (11.3.3). iOS and iPadOS are tightly Apple-controlled with consistent, low-fragmentation update deployment. Windows smartphones are discontinued and not a mobile platform.

Question 11

How does a knowledge base support the overall ticketing and incident resolution process in a support environment?

  • By tracking the status and ownership of tickets throughout the troubleshooting process
  • By escalating unresolved tickets to senior technicians or external support groups
  • By enabling users to resolve common issues independently before filing a ticket
  • By assigning severity levels to tickets based on their impact and urgency

A knowledge base supports the ticketing process primarily through deflection - users can find answers themselves without creating a ticket, reducing ticket volume. Ticket tracking, escalation, and prioritization are all functions of the ticketing system, not the knowledge base.

Question 12

Which of the following is true regarding vendor life-cycle limitations?

  • Vendors provide unlimited support for all versions of their operating systems.
  • Older operating system versions may lose support over time.
  • Operating system updates are always free and available for all devices.
  • Vendors guarantee compatibility of all hardware with every operating system version.

Per 11.3.7, vendor life cycles move products through supported, extended support, and end-of-life phases - after which no further security updates or support are provided. Vendor support is finite by design. Updates are not always free or available for all devices (older devices may lose support). New OS versions frequently introduce hardware requirements that exclude older devices, as with Windows 11's TPM 2.0 requirement.

Question 13

You are an IT support technician assisting a customer whose network connection is down, causing a critical disruption to their business operations. After initial troubleshooting, you identify that the issue is due to a faulty router. The customer is upset and insists that the router must be replaced immediately, but your company's policy requires approval from a supervisor before replacing hardware. How should you analyze the situation and proceed to collaborate with the customer and focus on a solution?

  • Attempt to repair the faulty router yourself, even if it is outside your expertise, to avoid escalating the issue or delaying the resolution.
  • Apologize to the customer for the inconvenience, but insist that the router cannot be replaced until the approval process is complete.
  • Explain the company policy to the customer and inform them that you will need to wait for supervisor approval before taking any further action.
  • Escalate the issue to your supervisor immediately, explain the urgency of the situation, and keep the customer informed while waiting for a response.

This answer satisfies both requirements: it follows company policy by obtaining proper authorization, and it applies professionalism principles of proactive escalation and keeping the customer informed during a critical incident (11.2.2, 11.2.7). Attempting repairs outside your expertise is unsafe and bypasses authorization. Apologizing or explaining without actively escalating leaves the customer in limbo during a critical outage - the key distinction is acting by escalating immediately, not passively waiting.

Question 14

What is the primary purpose of a knowledge base in a support environment?

  • To categorize and prioritize support tickets based on severity and impact.
  • To escalate unresolved tickets to senior technicians or third-party support groups.
  • To track the ownership and status of support tickets during the troubleshooting process.
  • To provide a collection of frequently asked questions (FAQs) and troubleshooting procedures for users.

A knowledge base is a self-service reference repository of FAQs, how-to guides, and documented solutions. Categorization, prioritization, escalation, and ticket tracking are all functions of the ticketing system, not the knowledge base.

Question 15

You are in the process of preparing a 128-GB flash drive and must determine which file system to use. Which of the following BEST describes the advantages of using the exFAT file system rather than NTFS on the flash drive?

  • Support for files over 4 GB
  • Support for volumes over 32 GB
  • Access control lists and permissions
  • Lower overhead and risk of corruption

exFAT was designed specifically for flash media with a simpler structure, fewer write operations, and lower overhead than NTFS - reducing flash wear and corruption risk. Both exFAT and NTFS support files over 4 GB and volumes well beyond 32 GB, so those are not differentiating advantages over NTFS. NTFS actually has more robust permissions support than exFAT, not less.
